## [Levitating magnetometer could detect ultrafaint brain signals](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02458-9) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers have developed a levitating magnetometer that uses a sensor magnet under one millimeter in size, suspended between a lifting magnet and a diamagnet, with a laser tracking its orientation. The device is sensitive enough to detect the faint magnetic fields produced by brain activity, offering a simpler alternative to complex existing technologies. This simple design could rival much more complex magnetometers used in biophysics, such as SQUIDs, potentially making brain activity detection more accessible and affordable. Additionally, its sensitivity might enable applications in fundamental physics, including searches for dark matter. The sensor magnet is levitated between a lifting magnet and a diamagnet, and a laser beam tracks its motion to measure magnetic fields. The device reduces the sample distance to a few hundred micrometers, enhancing sensitivity, and the study was published in Science.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 6, 00:00

**Background**: Magnetoencephalography (MEG) is a technique that maps brain activity by recording magnetic fields produced by electrical currents in the brain. Traditional MEG systems rely on highly sensitive magnetometers like SQUIDs (superconducting quantum interference devices), which require complex cryogenic cooling. The new levitating magnetometer offers a simpler, potentially room-temperature alternative, which could broaden the use of MEG in research and clinical settings.
