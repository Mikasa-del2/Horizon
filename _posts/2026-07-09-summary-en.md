## [Floquet Rotational Super-radiance Observed in Ring Resonators](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10725-y) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers experimentally observed Floquet rotational super-radiance in a spatiotemporally modulated ring resonator network, as reported in Nature on July 8, 2026. This is the first experimental demonstration of rotational super-radiance in a Floquet system, marking a major milestone in quantum optics and condensed matter physics. It opens new avenues for studying non-Hermitian and parametric dynamics in space-time structured media. The effect arises from parametric processes in dissipation-shaped spectral gaps that extract energy from the Floquet-rotating medium, leading to angular-momentum-selective amplification of orbital waves. The experiment used a ring network of time-modulated resonators.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 8, 00:00

**Background**: Super-radiance is a collective emission phenomenon where multiple emitters radiate coherently, enhancing the emission rate. Floquet systems are periodically driven systems that can exhibit exotic phases not present in static systems. Rotational super-radiance involves amplification of waves carrying orbital angular momentum, and its Floquet realization had been theoretically predicted but not observed until now.

## [hBN switches co-integrated on GaN for 6G mmWave circuits](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10761-8) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have co-integrated two-dimensional hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) switches directly onto gallium nitride (GaN) microchips to create programmable millimeter-wave monolithic microwave integrated circuits (MMICs) for 6G applications. This work was published in Nature on July 8, 2026. This breakthrough enables reconfigurable mmWave hardware, which is critical for 6G communication systems that require high-frequency, flexible, and energy-efficient components. Co-integrating 2D materials with GaN could lead to more compact and versatile RF front-ends. The hBN switches are monolayer films sandwiched between gold electrodes, exhibiting a cutoff-frequency figure of merit around 129 THz, low insertion loss (≤0.5 dB), high isolation (≥10 dB) from 0.1 to 200 GHz, and nanosecond switching speeds. The co-integration on GaN MMICs allows programmable circuit functions without external switches.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 8, 00:00

**Background**: Millimeter-wave (mmWave) frequencies (30–300 GHz) are essential for high-bandwidth 5G and future 6G networks. GaN is a wide-bandgap semiconductor widely used in high-power RF devices, while hBN is a 2D material known for its excellent insulating and switching properties. Monolithic microwave integrated circuits (MMICs) integrate all RF components on a single chip, and making them reconfigurable is a key goal for adaptive communication systems.

## [Cosmic protons enable Outer Space Treaty verification](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10783-2) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A Nature paper published on July 8, 2026, demonstrates that cosmic protons interacting with thermonuclear weapons in space produce detectable spallation neutrons, enabling verification of the Outer Space Treaty. This breakthrough provides a much-needed verification mechanism for the Outer Space Treaty, which bans nuclear weapons in space but currently lacks effective monitoring, addressing recent concerns about potential nuclear-armed anti-satellite weapons. The method uses widely available sensor technologies to observe neutrons induced by spallation from protons in the inner Van Allen radiation belts, and a shoebox-sized detector satellite could identify the telltale signal of a hidden nuclear device.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 8, 00:00

**Background**: The Outer Space Treaty of 1967 prohibits placing nuclear weapons in orbit, but no verification mechanism was established. Recent US concerns about Russia testing nuclear-armed anti-satellite components highlight the need for such verification. Cosmic ray spallation occurs when high-energy protons strike matter, ejecting neutrons; the Van Allen belts contain trapped protons that can induce this reaction in a nuclear warhead.
