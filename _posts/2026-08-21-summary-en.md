## [Radical-based method for dialkyl ether synthesis via het-SH2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11043-z) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Nature paper (published online 20 August 2026) reports a radical-based paradigm for general dialkyl ether synthesis, enabled by an underutilized heteroatom homolytic substitution (het-SH2) mechanism. This approach overcomes the steric limitations of classical SN2 and SN1 pathways. This advance addresses a longstanding challenge in drug discovery and medicinal chemistry: the modular and selective synthesis of dialkyl ethers, especially sterically congested variants. The new method could enable access to hindered alkyl ethers prevalent in bioactive natural products, with broad impact on synthetic organic chemistry. The paper highlights that classical SN2 reactions are fundamentally limited by steric congestion at the transition state, while SN1 pathways suffer from promiscuous carbocation intermediates prone to elimination, rearrangement, and loss of stereogenic information. The new het-SH2 mechanism offers a radical-based alternative that bypasses these issues.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 20, 00:00

**Background**: Dialkyl ethers are common motifs in pharmaceuticals and natural products, but their synthesis, especially for hindered variants, has been challenging. Traditional methods rely on nucleophilic substitution (SN2) or carbocation pathways (SN1), both of which have limitations under steric hindrance. Radical-based approaches, such as heteroatom homolytic substitution (het-SH2), offer a different mechanistic pathway that can potentially overcome these limitations.
