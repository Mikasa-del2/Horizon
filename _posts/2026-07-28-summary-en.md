## [Pyridoxal photoenzymes enable asymmetric radical cross-couplings](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10930-9) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have demonstrated that pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP), a vitamin B6 derivative, can function as a photoenzymatic cofactor to catalyze asymmetric radical–radical cross-couplings under visible light. This work, published in Nature on July 27, 2026, expands the known photoenzymatic cofactor repertoire beyond flavins and nicotinamides. This breakthrough addresses a long-standing challenge in organic synthesis: achieving enantioselective radical–radical couplings, which are difficult to control with traditional catalysts. The use of a benign vitamin-derived cofactor and visible light offers a green, sustainable route to chiral molecules important for pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals. The photoenzyme leverages an excited-state quinonoid intermediate of PLP as a potent single-electron reductant, enabling a redox-neutral cross-coupling between benzyl amines and reductive radical precursors. The reaction proceeds with high enantioselectivity and broad substrate scope, as demonstrated in the Nature paper.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 27, 00:00

**Background**: Photoenzymes use light to drive catalytic reactions, but only a few cofactors like flavins were known to support photoenzymatic activity. Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP) is a versatile coenzyme in nature, involved in transamination, decarboxylation, and racemization, but its photochemical potential was unexplored. Radical–radical cross-couplings are powerful yet challenging because radicals are highly reactive and prone to racemization; achieving asymmetry requires precise control of both radical generation and recombination.

## [Phase-homogeneous mixed halide perovskites for stable tandem solar cells](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10929-2) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have developed a method to produce phase-homogeneous mixed halide perovskites, suppressing phase segregation at the initial film formation stage, enabling stable wide-bandgap perovskites for tandem photovoltaics. The work was published in Nature on July 27, 2026. This breakthrough addresses a critical challenge in tandem solar cells—halide phase segregation—which has limited the stability and efficiency of wide-bandgap perovskites. It could accelerate the commercialization of high-efficiency tandem photovoltaics, potentially surpassing the efficiency limits of single-junction silicon cells. The method achieves phase homogeneity by controlling asymmetric nucleation of iodine and bromine during film formation, preventing segregation from the start. The resulting wide-bandgap perovskites maintain their bandgap longer in real devices, improving operational stability.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 27, 00:00

**Background**: Tandem solar cells stack multiple light-absorbing layers to capture a broader spectrum of sunlight, achieving higher efficiencies than single-junction cells. Mixed halide perovskites with tunable bandgaps are promising top-cell materials, but they suffer from halide phase segregation under illumination, which degrades performance. This work suppresses segregation at the nucleation stage, a novel approach.

## [Brain 'Timer' Tracks Sleep and Predicts Awakening](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02294-x) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study in mice published in Nature on July 27, 2026, suggests that chemical tags on proteins in the brain could serve as a biomarker for sleep deprivation and even predict when an animal will wake up. This finding could lead to the development of objective diagnostic tools for sleep deprivation in humans, which currently relies on subjective self-reporting. Understanding the molecular 'timer' may also deepen insights into the fundamental mechanisms of sleep regulation. The chemical tags are likely post-translational modifications on proteins, such as phosphorylation or acetylation, that accumulate during wakefulness and are cleared during sleep. The study tracked these changes in mouse brains and correlated them with sleep pressure and the timing of spontaneous awakening.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 27, 00:00

**Background**: Sleep deprivation is known to impair cognitive function and health, but there is no reliable molecular biomarker to measure sleep debt. Proteomics, the large-scale study of proteins, has been used to identify changes in blood proteins after sleep loss, but brain-specific markers have been elusive. This study provides evidence for a brain-based 'sleep timer' at the protein level.
