This scalable electrodeposition method could enable cost-effective, large-area manufacturing of high-efficiency perovskite solar cells, addressing a key barrier to commercialization. ## [Crustc Transpiles Entire Rust Compiler to C](https://github.com/FractalFir/crustc) ⭐️ 8.0/10

FractalFir's crustc project has successfully transpiled the entire rustc compiler to C, enabling Rust to run on platforms without LLVM or GCC support. This breakthrough solves Rust's bootstrapping problem on obscure hardware and enhances security verification through diverse double-compiling (DDC). Crustc is the 14th known attempt to compile Rust to C, and it leverages GCC's optimizer for performance, unlike LLVM's deprecated C backend.

hackernews · Philpax · Jul 2, 22:57 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48768464)

**Background**: Bootstrapping is the process of using an older compiler to compile a newer version of itself. Rust currently requires a pre-built rustc binary to compile from source, which limits its portability. Crustc addresses this by producing a C translation that can be compiled by any C compiler, enabling Rust to run on platforms without LLVM or GCC.

## [Neutrino Traced to Star-Forming Galaxy 'Shadow Blaster'](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02034-1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Astrophysicists have traced a high-energy neutrino detected at the South Pole's IceCube observatory to a specific star-forming galaxy in the early universe, nicknamed the 'Shadow Blaster'. This marks the first time a neutrino has been linked to a galaxy of this type. This discovery represents a major breakthrough in multi-messenger astronomy, demonstrating that star-forming galaxies can produce high-energy neutrinos. It opens a new window into understanding cosmic particle accelerators and the early universe. The neutrino was detected by IceCube, a cubic-kilometer neutrino detector buried in Antarctic ice. The 'Shadow Blaster' galaxy is a distant, star-forming galaxy from when the universe was young, and its identification was made possible by combining neutrino data with electromagnetic observations.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 2, 00:00

**Background**: Neutrinos are nearly massless, weakly interacting particles that can travel across the universe without being deflected, making them ideal messengers from distant cosmic sources. Multi-messenger astronomy combines different signals—such as light, gravitational waves, and neutrinos—to gain a more complete understanding of astrophysical events. IceCube is designed to detect high-energy neutrinos from astrophysical sources.
