## [Ada Yonath Obituary: Ribosome Crystallography Pioneer Dies](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02801-0) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Nature published an obituary for Ada Yonath, the Nobel laureate who died recently, highlighting her pioneering work in mapping the ribosome structure despite widespread skepticism. Her development of cryo-bio-crystallography overcame the 'impossible' challenge of crystallizing fragile ribosomes. Yonath's work revolutionized structural biology by enabling high-resolution studies of ribosomes, which are essential for protein synthesis and antibiotic development. Her techniques have become routine in the field, impacting drug discovery and our understanding of genetic diseases. Yonath introduced cryo-bio-crystallography, which involves cooling crystals to very low temperatures to protect them from radiation damage, allowing structural determination of the 70S ribosome, a complex of 52 proteins and 3 RNA molecules with a molecular mass of ~2.5 million Daltons. She was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2009 for her work on ribosome structure.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Sep 4, 00:00

**Background**: Ribosomes are cellular machines that synthesize proteins by translating messenger RNA. Before Yonath's work, it was thought impossible to crystallize ribosomes because they are large, complex, and fragile. Her persistence and innovative cryo-crystallography methods overcame these obstacles, paving the way for detailed structural studies that have been crucial for understanding protein synthesis and developing antibiotics.

## [Palladium-Catalyzed Decarboxylative Rearrangement Enables Ketone Homologation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11091-5) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A novel palladium-catalysed decarboxylative rearrangement has been developed for ketone homologation, as reported in Nature. This method provides a closed-shell pathway that enables precise stereochemical control, with the migrating carbon retaining its configuration and the α-stereocenter undergoing inversion. This advance offers a powerful synthetic tool for constructing homologated ketones, which are valuable intermediates in pharmaceutical and materials chemistry. It may overcome limitations of classical methods, such as the Tiffeneau–Demjanov and Büchner–Curtius–Schlotterbeck reactions, by providing higher migrating-group selectivity and better stereocontrol. The reaction is a palladium-catalysed decarboxylative rearrangement, which proceeds via a closed-shell pathway, avoiding stepwise radical or carbocationic intermediates that cause side reactions. For unsymmetrical ketones, it exhibits markedly higher migrating-group selectivity compared to classical reactions.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Sep 3, 00:00

**Background**: Ketone homologation is a chemical process that extends a ketone by one or more carbon atoms, typically by inserting a methylene group. Classical methods include reactions with diazo compounds or the Tiffeneau–Demjanov rearrangement. Decarboxylative rearrangements involve the loss of carbon dioxide and simultaneous migration of a group, often used in synthesis to form new carbon-carbon bonds.
