## [Modular in vivo click converts antibodies to ADCs to reverse drug resistance](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10789-w) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers developed a modular in vivo click chemistry method that converts therapeutic antibodies into antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) directly within the body, reversing drug resistance in heterogeneous tumours. This approach enables dynamic, on-demand ADC formation from existing antibodies, potentially overcoming tumour heterogeneity and acquired resistance that limit current ADC therapies. The method uses bioorthogonal copper-free click chemistry to ligate a drug payload to antibodies pre-targeted to tumour cells, achieving enhanced antitumour activity in treatment-resistant models.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 15, 00:00

**Background**: Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are targeted cancer therapies that deliver cytotoxic drugs to tumour cells via monoclonal antibodies. However, tumour heterogeneity and drug resistance often limit their efficacy. Bioorthogonal click chemistry, recognized by the 2022 Nobel Prize, allows selective chemical reactions in living systems without interfering with native biology.

## [Queen Naked Mole-Rat Pheromone Suppresses Reproduction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10772-5) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers discovered that naked mole-rat queens produce isopropyl myristate, a low-volatility ester that suppresses reproduction in other females, thereby maintaining eusocial hierarchy. The study was published in Nature on July 15, 2026. This is the first identification of a specific chemical mechanism for reproductive suppression in a eusocial mammal, offering insights into evolutionary biology and neuroendocrinology. It could also inform studies on social behavior and chemical communication in other species. Isopropyl myristate is a low-volatility ester that high-ranking animals detect and avoid; it alters prolactin and progesterone levels in other females to suppress reproduction. The compound is commonly used in cosmetics and pharmaceuticals as a skin penetration enhancer.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 15, 00:00

**Background**: Naked mole-rats (Heterocephalus glaber) are eusocial mammals, living in colonies with a single breeding queen and non-reproductive workers. Reproductive suppression is common in eusocial species, but the chemical signals involved were previously unknown. Isopropyl myristate is a synthetic ester used in cosmetics, but its natural role as a pheromone is newly discovered.

## [Universal quantum gates via anyon braiding and fusion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10709-y) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrated universal topological quantum computation on a 54-qubit quantum processor by combining braiding and fusion of non-Abelian anyons, realizing non-Abelian S3 topological order. This is a groundbreaking experimental milestone that proves universal topological quantum computation is achievable, potentially leading to fault-tolerant quantum computers resistant to decoherence. The experiment used a 54-qubit superconducting processor to create non-Abelian S3 topological order, where anyon braiding alone is insufficient for universality, but adding fusion operations completes the universal gate set.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 15, 00:00

**Background**: Topological quantum computation uses anyons—quasiparticles with exotic statistics—to encode and process quantum information in a way that is inherently protected from local errors. Non-Abelian anyons have braiding operations that can implement quantum gates, but for universal computation, fusion operations are also required. This work combines both on a real quantum processor.

## [Quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystals filter light by photon statistics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10782-3) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have demonstrated a new class of materials called quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystals that selectively transmit light based on its quantum coherence, creating bands of allowed and forbidden photon statistics. The work was published in Nature on July 15, 2026. This breakthrough enables filtering light by its quantum statistical properties rather than wavelength or polarization, which could revolutionize photonics and quantum technologies. It opens new possibilities for quantum information processing, single-photon sources, and fundamental studies of light-matter interaction. The metacrystal is a nanostructured gold surface that filters light depending on the tendency of its photons to arrive at a detector in pairs, effectively sorting light by its quantum statistics. The material exhibits bands where certain photon statistics are allowed or forbidden, analogous to electronic bandgaps in semiconductors.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 15, 00:00

**Background**: Photon statistics describe the correlation between photon arrivals at a detector, with common types including Poissonian (coherent light), super-Poissonian (thermal light), and sub-Poissonian (nonclassical light). Plasmonic metamaterials are engineered structures that exploit surface plasmons to manipulate light at the nanoscale, but previous designs did not exploit quantum coherence for filtering. This work combines quantum optics with plasmonics to create a filter that responds to the quantum state of light.

## [Scalable quasi-pure MOF membranes achieve near-intrinsic gas separation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10655-9) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have developed scalable quasi-pure metal-organic framework (MOF) membranes that achieve near-intrinsic separation performance for gas separations, as published in Nature on July 15, 2026. This breakthrough enables energy-efficient industrial gas separations with major cost reductions compared to traditional distillation, potentially transforming industries such as petrochemicals and natural gas processing. The membranes are termed 'quasi-pure' because they consist predominantly of MOF material with minimal defects, overcoming previous challenges in fabricating continuous pure MOF membranes. The near-intrinsic performance means the membranes operate close to the theoretical maximum selectivity and permeability of the MOF material.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 15, 00:00

**Background**: Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) are porous crystalline materials with uniform pore sizes, making them ideal for membrane-based gas separations. However, MOFs typically form as powders, and fabricating them into continuous defect-free membranes has been difficult. Previous approaches like mixed matrix membranes (MMMs) often suffer from poor compatibility between MOF fillers and polymer matrices, leading to defects that reduce performance.

Researchers have demonstrated that elastic instabilities in mechanical metamaterials can be harnessed to program fracture behavior, achieving up to a tenfold increase in fracture energy. This work transforms instability from a failure mechanism into a design principle. This paradigm shift enables the design of metamaterials with unprecedented fracture resistance, which could impact aerospace, automotive, and protective equipment industries. It opens new avenues for creating materials that are both lightweight and tough. The study, published in Nature on July 15, 2026, uses a two-dimensional spring-lattice metamaterial to decouple fracture toughness from deformation stiffness. The approach leverages elastic instabilities that were traditionally considered failure precursors.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 15, 00:00

**Background**: Mechanical metamaterials are engineered structures that derive unusual properties from their architecture rather than composition. Elastic instabilities, such as buckling, typically lead to failure, but this research shows they can be programmed to enhance fracture resistance. Fracture energy measures the energy absorbed during crack propagation, and increasing it is key to tougher materials.

## [Sleep Regularity Tops Duration in Predicting Mortality Risk](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/47/1/zsad253/7280269) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A 2023 study published in *Sleep* found that sleep regularity—consistency of sleep-wake timing—is a stronger predictor of all-cause mortality risk than sleep duration. This challenges the common focus on sleep duration alone and suggests that maintaining a consistent sleep schedule may be more critical for longevity, with implications for public health guidelines and clinical practice. The study analyzed data from over 60,000 participants in the UK Biobank, using accelerometer-derived sleep regularity index (SRI). Higher SRI (more regular sleep) was associated with lower mortality, independent of sleep duration and other confounders.

**Background**: Sleep regularity refers to the consistency of an individual's sleep-wake timing from day to day, often measured by the sleep regularity index (SRI). Previous research has largely focused on sleep duration, but emerging evidence suggests that circadian rhythm disruption may have independent health effects. This study adds to that evidence by directly comparing regularity and duration as mortality predictors.
