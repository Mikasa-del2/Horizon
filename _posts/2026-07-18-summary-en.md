## [Sperm production restored after 16-year-old frozen testicular tissue transplant](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02191-3) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A man who had testicular tissue frozen at age 10 before chemotherapy received an autologous transplant 16 years later, and it successfully restored his sperm production. This is the first reported case of functional recovery using long-term cryopreserved prepubertal testicular tissue in humans. This breakthrough offers a potential fertility preservation option for prepubertal boys facing gonadotoxic cancer treatments, who currently have no established method to preserve fertility. It could transform reproductive medicine by enabling future biological parenthood for childhood cancer survivors. The tissue was cryopreserved for 16 years before being transplanted back into the patient after he completed chemotherapy. The procedure is still experimental and not widely available, with ongoing research needed to optimize techniques and assess long-term safety.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 17, 00:00

**Background**: Cryopreservation of testicular tissue is an experimental method proposed to preserve fertility in prepubertal boys who cannot yet produce sperm for standard sperm banking. When these boys later wish to father children, the frozen tissue can be thawed and transplanted back (autologous transplantation) to potentially restore sperm production. Until now, successful restoration of spermatogenesis from long-term frozen prepubertal tissue had only been demonstrated in animal models.

This poses a significant security risk as it allows a third-party vendor to install software with full system access, potentially enabling supply chain attacks. The installation occurs automatically when a monitor is connected via HDMI, and the software runs at every system boot with no sandboxing.

**Background**: Windows Update is designed to deliver driver and software updates from hardware manufacturers. However, this feature can be abused to push arbitrary software, as seen with LG monitors and previously with Gigabyte motherboards.

This breakthrough enables unobtrusive, long-term monitoring of neural activity and physiological signals, advancing brain-computer interfaces and health monitoring without social stigma or discomfort. The electrodes are designed to be undetectable by both eye and touch, and can be worn on the face to measure electroencephalography (EEG) and other signals. The technology was published in Science in July 2026.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 17, 00:00

This new work overcomes those challenges with materials that match skin properties.

## [First True Sugar Molecule Found in Space](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02263-4) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Astronomers have detected erythrulose, a four-carbon sugar molecule, in interstellar space near the center of our Galaxy. Additionally, a formula on a Maya wall has revealed the name of an eighth-century mathematician, Sak Tahn Waax. This is the first detection of a 'true sugar' in space, providing clues about the prebiotic chemistry that may have led to life on Earth. The Maya discovery highlights the sophistication of ancient astronomy and mathematics. Erythrulose is a chiral ketose sugar, commonly found on Earth in raspberries. The Maya formula relates time periods of celestial motions, and the name Sak Tahn Waax translates to 'White-chested Fox'.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 17, 00:00

**Background**: Sugars are essential for life, but their presence in interstellar space has been elusive due to detection challenges. Erythrulose was identified using ultrasensitive spectral surveys of the Galactic Center. The Maya text, from an eighth-century mural, is one of the few known examples of a named mathematician in Classic Maya culture.

## [Giant Space Mirrors to Boost Green Energy?](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02218-9) ⭐️ 6.0/10

California startup Reflect Orbital has received FCC approval to test a satellite with a 60-foot mirror that would reflect sunlight to Earth, with plans to eventually deploy 50,000 such satellites to provide 'sunlight on demand' for solar farms. If successful, this could extend solar power generation into nighttime and increase renewable energy output, but astronomers warn the massive satellite constellation would severely interfere with ground-based telescopes and dark-sky observations. The mirror-bearing satellites would orbit at about 600 km altitude and reflect sunlight to targeted areas on Earth for several minutes at a time. The FCC approval is only for a single test satellite, and the full constellation faces significant regulatory and technical hurdles.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 17, 00:00

**Background**: Space-based solar power (SBSP) typically involves collecting solar energy in orbit and beaming it to Earth via microwaves. Reflect Orbital's approach is different: it uses mirrors to simply reflect sunlight, not convert it. Previous SBSP demonstrations, like Caltech's MAPLE, transmitted only microwatts of power. The concept of orbital mirrors for illumination has been discussed for decades but never deployed at scale.
