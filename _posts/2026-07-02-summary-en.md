## [Isomeric hydrogen bonding boosts blue perovskite LED efficiency](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10723-0) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers achieved a record external quantum efficiency (EQE) of 22.0% for blue perovskite LEDs by engineering isomeric multi-hydrogen-bonding networks, as reported in Nature on July 1, 2026. This breakthrough addresses a long-standing challenge in display technology—achieving efficient and stable blue emission from perovskites—paving the way for full-color perovskite displays with performance comparable to existing technologies. The isomeric multi-hydrogen-bonding approach stabilizes the perovskite structure and suppresses non-radiative recombination, enabling saturated blue emission with high efficiency. The EQE of 22.0% is the highest reported for blue perovskite LEDs to date.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 1, 00:00

**Background**: Perovskite light-emitting diodes (PeLEDs) are promising for next-generation displays due to their high color purity and low cost, but blue PeLEDs have lagged behind red and green counterparts in efficiency and stability. Hydrogen bonding can influence crystal growth and defect passivation, and using isomeric molecules allows precise control of the bonding network.

## [Direct measurement of carrier transfer length in 2D transistors](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10707-0) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers used cross-sectional scanning tunnelling microscopy to directly measure a carrier transfer length of approximately 2.0 nm in bismuth-contacted monolayer MoS2 transistors, as reported in Nature on July 1, 2026. This measurement defines the fundamental scaling limit for metal contacts in sub-10 nm 2D electronic devices, which is critical for the continued miniaturization of transistors beyond silicon. The carrier transfer length was measured at the contact region of a bismuth-contacted monolayer MoS2 transistor using cross-sectional scanning tunnelling microscopy, revealing a value of about 2.0 nm.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 1, 00:00

**Background**: In field-effect transistors, the carrier transfer length at the metal-semiconductor contact determines how efficiently charge carriers move from the metal into the semiconductor. For 2D materials like MoS2, achieving low-resistance contacts is challenging due to Fermi level pinning and Schottky barriers. Bismuth has been shown to form ohmic contacts with MoS2, enabling better device performance.

## [N4-acetylcytidine boosts synthetic mRNA translation yield and fidelity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10729-8) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A study published in Nature on July 1, 2026, demonstrates that the RNA modification N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C) enhances both the yield and fidelity of synthetic mRNA translation compared to the current industry standard N1-methylpseudouridine (m1Ψ). This finding challenges the dominant use of m1Ψ in mRNA therapeutics and vaccines, potentially leading to more effective and safer synthetic mRNA products with higher protein output and fewer errors. The study shows that different RNA modifications elicit different translation elongation rates, with ac4C providing superior translation output and fidelity over m1Ψ. The research was published in Nature with doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10729-8.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 1, 00:00

**Background**: N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C) is a naturally occurring post-transcriptional modification found in tRNAs, rRNAs, and mRNAs across all domains of life. In contrast, N1-methylpseudouridine (m1Ψ) is a modified nucleoside used in SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccines like those from Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna. Translation elongation rate is a key factor influencing protein synthesis efficiency and accuracy.
