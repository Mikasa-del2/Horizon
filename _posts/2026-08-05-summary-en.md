## [Cold-atom simulator maps pseudogap in Fermi-Hubbard model](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10875-z) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A research team has used a cold-atom quantum simulator to directly observe and map the pseudogap metallic phase in the Fermi-Hubbard model, as reported in Nature on August 5, 2026. This marks a significant experimental milestone in simulating strongly correlated electron systems. This breakthrough provides unprecedented insight into the pseudogap phase, which is widely considered essential for understanding high-temperature superconductivity in cuprates and other doped Mott insulators. The ability to simulate and probe this phase with cold atoms opens new avenues for designing materials with improved superconducting properties. The experiment used ultracold fermionic atoms in an optical lattice to realize the Hubbard model, and the pseudogap was identified through momentum-resolved photoemission spectroscopy. The phase diagram was mapped as a function of doping and temperature, revealing the extent of the pseudogap region.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 5, 00:00

**Background**: The Fermi-Hubbard model is a fundamental model in condensed matter physics that describes interacting electrons on a lattice, capturing essential physics of doped Mott insulators and high-temperature superconductors. The pseudogap is a mysterious phase where the density of electronic states is suppressed near the Fermi level, yet the system remains metallic. Cold-atom quantum simulators offer a highly controllable platform to study such strongly correlated systems, complementing solid-state experiments.

It will aid researchers in characterizing putative human transcription factors and improve the accuracy of regulatory genomics analyses. The study used a panel of assays that analyze different aspects of DNA sequence specificity, yielding more than 100 new motifs. ## [Ubiquitous Kelvin-Helmholtz Instabilities Drive Plasma Mixing on the Sun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10871-3) ⭐️ 8.0/10

High-spatial-resolution observations of the solar photosphere, published in Nature on August 5, 2026, reveal that Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities are ubiquitous and drive plasma mixing on the Sun. This finding challenges previous models of solar plasma dynamics and could reshape our understanding of energy transport and heating in the solar atmosphere, impacting solar physics and space weather forecasting. The observations were made with high-spatial-resolution instruments, revealing deformed magnetic boundaries and ultra-fine dark striations at scales of tens of kilometers, which are signatures of the Kelvin-Helmholtz instability.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 5, 00:00

**Background**: The Kelvin-Helmholtz instability occurs when there is a velocity shear between two fluids or plasma layers, leading to wave growth and eventual mixing. In the solar context, it has been observed in jets and boundaries, but this study shows it is ubiquitous in the photosphere, suggesting a more dynamic and complex solar surface than previously thought.
