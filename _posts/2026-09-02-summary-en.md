## [Bacterial ribosomes can translate proteins from their own RNA](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10962-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have demonstrated that bacterial ribosomes can synthesize proteins encoded within their own ribosomal RNA (rRNA), a finding published in Nature on September 2, 2026. This challenges the long-held assumption that ribosomes only translate external messenger RNA (mRNA). This paradigm-shifting discovery could reshape our understanding of translation and molecular evolution, and may open new avenues in synthetic biology for engineering ribosomes with novel functions. It also raises fundamental questions about the origin of life and the ribosome's role as a self-replicating entity. The study shows that bacterial ribosomes can translate proteins from their own rRNA, but the exact mechanism and efficiency remain to be fully characterized. The research was published in Nature with the DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10962-1, indicating high technical depth and novelty.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Sep 2, 00:00

**Background**: Ribosomes are complex molecular machines composed of ribosomal RNA and proteins, responsible for translating messenger RNA into proteins in all living cells. Traditionally, translation is understood as the decoding of mRNA by ribosomes, with the ribosome itself not being a template for protein synthesis. This discovery suggests that ribosomes may have self-referential capabilities, potentially related to the 'selfish ribosome' hypothesis, which posits that the ribosome may act as a selfish genetic element.

## [First Glimpse of Dark Matter? Energy Flash Hints at WIMP](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01985-9) ⭐️ 9.0/10

An experiment has detected an energy flash consistent with a weakly interacting massive particle (WIMP), potentially marking the first direct observation of dark matter. The finding was published online in Nature on September 1, 2026. If confirmed, this would be a groundbreaking discovery in physics, providing the first direct evidence of dark matter and opening a new window into the universe's composition. It could validate the WIMP hypothesis and reshape our understanding of particle physics and cosmology. The experiment observed an energy flash similar to that expected from a WIMP, but the result has not yet been independently verified. The specific experiment name and location were not disclosed in the news item.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Sep 1, 00:00

**Background**: Dark matter is an invisible substance that makes up about 27% of the universe, yet it does not emit, absorb, or reflect light. WIMPs are a leading candidate for dark matter particles; they interact only through gravity and the weak nuclear force, making them extremely difficult to detect. Experiments typically search for the faint signals produced when a WIMP collides with an atomic nucleus.

A study published in Nature on September 2, 2026, reveals that inflammation triggers interchromosomal DNA interactions, bringing parent genes into proximity and facilitating the formation of chimeric mRNAs that encode functional proteins involved in mammalian immunity. This discovery unveils a novel mechanism of gene expression regulation in immunity, potentially reshaping our understanding of how the immune system responds to inflammation. It could open new avenues for therapeutic interventions targeting chimeric mRNAs or the underlying chromatin interactions. The study demonstrates that chimeric mRNAs are not merely transcriptional noise but encode physiologically relevant proteins. The mechanism involves interchromosomal interactions that are induced by inflammatory signals, suggesting a dynamic and regulated process.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Sep 2, 00:00

**Background**: Chimeric RNAs are formed by the fusion of genetic material from two or more distinct genes, often resulting from chromosomal rearrangements or trans-splicing. Interchromosomal interactions are part of the 3D genome organization and have been implicated in gene expression regulation. This study provides evidence that such interactions can be dynamically induced by inflammation to produce functional chimeric mRNAs.

## [Connectomics reveals how distributed plasticity enables sensory prediction in electric fish](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10690-6) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new Nature study published online on September 2, 2026, combines connectomics, electrophysiology, and modeling to show that distributed synaptic plasticity across multiple network layers in the cerebellum-like electrosensory lobe (ELL) of weakly electric fish cooperates to enable fast, accurate, and noise-robust sensory prediction. This work provides unprecedented insight into how neural circuits implement predictive learning at the synaptic level, with implications for understanding cerebellar function and for designing biologically inspired AI systems that learn efficiently from noisy sensory streams. The study leverages a comprehensive connectome of the ELL to map cell types and synaptic connections underlying predictive sensory processing. A computational model constrained by prior electrophysiological recordings shows how structured feedforward and recurrent connectivity supports cancellation of predictable electrosensory input.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Sep 2, 00:00

**Background**: Weakly electric fish generate electric fields to sense their environment, and they must cancel self-generated signals to detect external stimuli. The cerebellum-like ELL is a well-characterized circuit for predictive sensory processing, where synaptic plasticity is thought to underlie learning. Connectomics provides a complete map of neural connections, enabling detailed circuit analysis.
