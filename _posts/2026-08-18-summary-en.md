## [Google Buys Spirit Airlines' Data in Bankruptcy Auction](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/18/google-buys-crashed-airline-spirits-data-at-auction-because-ai/5288962) ⭐️ 8.0/10

This deal raises significant privacy and ethical concerns, as the massive dataset may contain personal information that could be repurposed for AI training without clear consent. It highlights the growing value of personal data and the potential for re-identification even after de-identification efforts. A third-party 'Deidentification Agent' will strip personal identifiers before handing data to Google, but experts doubt the effectiveness.

## [Railway Network as Flatbed Scanner: Train-Mounted Line Camera](https://philo.gay/linecam/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A creative project by philo.gay uses an industrial linear scanning camera mounted on trains and ferries to capture extremely wide panoramic images, stitching thousands of lines per second into coherent photos. The project was presented at EMF 2026 and published on August 17, 2026, with an example image of 56,894x2,048 pixels taken on the San Francisco to Oakland ferry. This project demonstrates a novel and accessible approach to large-scale panoramic photography, turning everyday transportation into a creative tool. It inspires both artists and technologists to explore unconventional uses of hardware and computer vision, potentially influencing future creative coding and photography projects. The project involves measuring train speed and collecting lines fast enough to produce a coherent image, as well as processing and displaying extremely wide images. The author notes the challenge of stitching several thousand lines per second, and the results are grayscale images with high resolution, such as the 56,894x2,048 pixel ferry image.

hackernews · otherayden · Aug 18, 12:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49344825)

**Background**: Line scan cameras build an image one horizontal line at a time, unlike traditional frame cameras that capture a full frame at once. This technique is commonly used in industrial inspection, but this project applies it creatively to moving trains and ferries, effectively using the railway network as a flatbed scanner. The concept is similar to slit-scan photography, where a narrow slit captures a continuous strip of the scene over time.
