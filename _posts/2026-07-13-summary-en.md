## [Photo-transformable stabilizer boosts tandem solar cell stability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10869-x) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have developed a photo-transformable stabilizer that significantly improves the stability and efficiency of perovskite-organic tandem solar cells, as published in Nature on July 13, 2026. This breakthrough addresses a key stability challenge in perovskite-organic tandem solar cells, which are promising for high-efficiency photovoltaics, potentially accelerating their commercial adoption. The stabilizer is photo-transformable, meaning it changes its structure under light exposure to protect the perovskite layer from degradation, particularly addressing halide segregation in wide-bandgap mixed-halide perovskites.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 13, 00:00

**Background**: Perovskite-organic tandem solar cells combine a wide-bandgap perovskite front cell with an organic rear cell to capture more sunlight. However, mixed-halide perovskites with high bromine content suffer from initial halide segregation under illumination, reducing stability. This work introduces a stabilizer that transforms under light to mitigate this issue.

## [Epigenetic Editing Advances for Precise Gene Control](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02151-x) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers are advancing epigenetic editing techniques to rewrite chemical tags on DNA and chromatin, enabling precise control of gene expression without altering the DNA sequence. This breakthrough could revolutionize medicine by allowing targeted activation or silencing of disease-related genes, and it provides a powerful tool for studying gene regulation in biology. Epigenetic editing uses engineered DNA-binding domains (e.g., zinc fingers, TALEs, or dCas9) fused to effector domains that modify epigenetic marks like DNA methylation and histone modifications.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 13, 00:00

**Background**: Epigenetic editing is a type of genetic engineering that modifies the epigenome—chemical tags on DNA and histones—without changing the DNA sequence. Unlike gene editing (e.g., CRISPR-Cas9), which alters the DNA itself, epigenetic editing modulates gene expression by adding or removing epigenetic marks. This approach allows for reversible and potentially safer regulation of genes.

## [First True Sugar Molecule Found in Space](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02173-5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Astronomers have detected erythrulose, a four-carbon sugar, in interstellar space for the first time, making it the most complex sugar found beyond our Solar System. This discovery supports the theory that complex organic molecules, including sugars essential for life, can form in space and may have been delivered to early Earth, potentially seeding life's origins. Erythrulose is a ketotetrose with the formula C4H8O4, also found in red raspberries and used in self-tanning products. The detection was made using radio telescopes observing the interstellar medium.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 13, 00:00

**Background**: Sugars are fundamental building blocks of life, forming the backbone of RNA and DNA. Previous detections in space included simpler sugars like glycolaldehyde, but erythrulose is the first true sugar (a carbohydrate with a ketone group) found in interstellar space. The interstellar medium contains thin clouds of gas and dust where complex molecules can form.

## [Tall and small trees equally vulnerable to drought](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02121-3) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A study on dipterocarps in Borneo reveals that both tall and small trees are equally vulnerable to drought, challenging the long-held assumption that taller trees are more susceptible to water stress. This finding reshapes our understanding of forest dynamics under climate change, suggesting that drought impacts may be more uniform across tree sizes than previously thought, with implications for forest conservation and carbon storage predictions. The study focused on dipterocarps, which can exceed 70 meters in height, and used tree climbers to measure water transport and stress responses. The water-transport system of these giants has evolved to defy gravity and resist water stress, enabling tall trees to remain as drought-resilient as smaller ones.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 13, 00:00

**Background**: Dipterocarps are a family of tropical trees that dominate Southeast Asian rainforests, with some species reaching over 90 meters tall. Previous ecological theory suggested that taller trees face greater hydraulic challenges due to gravity and longer transport distances, making them more vulnerable to drought. This study directly tested that assumption by comparing drought responses across tree heights.
