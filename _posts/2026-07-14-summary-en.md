Researchers have repurposed a bacterial self-destruct mechanism into a CRISPR-based enzyme that selectively shreds DNA in cancer cells, showing efficacy in mouse models. The enzyme detects cancer-specific genetic signatures and shreds chromatin, the DNA-protein complex that forms chromosomes, leading to cell death. The study was published in Nature on July 14, 2026.

## [Photo-transformable stabilizer boosts perovskite-organic tandem solar cells](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10869-x) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers introduced a photo-transformable additive, 4-[3-(trifluoromethyl)-3H-diazirin-3-yl]benzylamine (TDB), into wide-bandgap perovskite precursor solutions to stabilize mixed-halide phases in perovskite-organic tandem solar cells. This two-stage strategy addresses initial halide-mixing inhomogeneity and light-induced halide segregation, improving device stability and performance. This breakthrough addresses a critical stability issue in perovskite-organic tandem solar cells, which are promising for achieving efficiencies beyond 30% with low carbon emissions. The photo-transformable stabilizer could accelerate the commercialization of high-efficiency tandem photovoltaics. The study was published in Nature on July 13, 2026, and the TDB additive undergoes photo-transformation upon light exposure to form a stabilizing network within the perovskite film. This method specifically targets wide-bandgap mixed-halide perovskites with high bromine content, which are prone to halide segregation.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 13, 00:00

**Background**: Perovskite-organic tandem solar cells combine a wide-bandgap perovskite front cell with a narrow-bandgap organic back cell to capture more sunlight. However, mixed-halide perovskites with high bromine content suffer from light-induced halide segregation, which degrades performance over time. The photo-transformable stabilizer offers a new way to lock the halide distribution in place.

## [First True Sugar Molecule Found in Space](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02173-5) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Astronomers have detected erythrulose, a four-carbon sugar, in an interstellar cloud, marking the most complex sugar ever found beyond our Solar System. This discovery provides direct evidence that complex organic molecules essential for life can form in space, supporting the theory that life's building blocks may have extraterrestrial origins. Erythrulose is a chiral ketose sugar, and its detection suggests that sugars can form efficiently on icy dust grains in interstellar clouds via grain-surface chemistry.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 13, 00:00

**Background**: Sugars are fundamental to life as components of DNA, RNA, and cellular energy. Previously, only simpler sugars like glycolaldehyde (a two-carbon sugar) had been detected in space. Erythrulose, with four carbons, is a true sugar and a step closer to the complexity needed for life.

## [Epigenetic editing advances to precisely rewrite DNA tags](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02151-x) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers are advancing epigenetic editing techniques to precisely rewrite chemical tags on DNA and chromatin, enabling tunable gene expression without altering the DNA sequence itself. This breakthrough could revolutionize gene therapy and synthetic biology by offering a reversible and safer alternative to traditional gene editing, with potential applications in treating diseases caused by dysregulated gene expression. The approach uses engineered DNA-binding domains such as zinc finger proteins, TALEs, or nuclease-deficient Cas9 (dCas9) fused to epigenetic effector domains to modify specific genomic loci.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Jul 13, 00:00

**Background**: Epigenetic editing is a type of genetic engineering that modifies the epigenome—chemical tags on DNA and chromatin that influence gene activity—without changing the DNA sequence. Unlike CRISPR-based gene editing which cuts DNA, epigenetic editing alters gene expression by adding or removing methyl groups or histone modifications. This allows researchers to study the functional role of specific epigenetic marks and potentially correct aberrant gene silencing or activation in disease.

This policy incentivizes shifting energy use to midday when solar generation peaks, reducing grid strain and lowering system costs for all consumers. It also boosts the economics of home batteries and grid-scale storage. The free electricity window is typically 11 a.m. to 2 p.m., and the offer is optional for households—customers must actively switch to such a plan.
