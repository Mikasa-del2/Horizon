## [JFrog: Many 'Critical' SQLite CVEs Are LLM-Generated False Positives](https://research.jfrog.com/post/sqlite-critical-cves-or-llm-slops/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

JFrog Security Research published an analysis revealing that a batch of SQLite CVEs rated as 'critical' are actually false positives generated by LLMs. The report shows these CVEs lack vendor corroboration, commit history, and contain metadata contradictions, indicating they were fabricated by AI. This matters because LLM-generated false positives pollute vulnerability databases, wasting organizations' time and resources on investigating and patching non-existent vulnerabilities. It also raises concerns about the reliability of AI in security reporting and the potential for abuse by malicious actors. The JFrog analysis found that none of the flagged CVEs appear on SQLite's official advisory page, and testing the advisories with Gptzero triggered AI-generated content warnings. The report highlights missing vendor corroboration, absent commit history, and empty CPE product definitions as red flags.

LLMs, or large language models, are AI systems that generate text based on statistical patterns, and they can produce plausible but incorrect content, known as 'hallucinations'. The increasing use of LLMs in security research has led to concerns about the quality and trustworthiness of automated vulnerability reports.

By leveraging gasdermin D's pore-forming ability, it offers a precise way to deliver inhibitors directly to the site of pyroptosis, potentially reducing side effects. A Nature article published on August 3, 2026, highlights the rise of 'review mills'—researchers who produce fake referee reports and make coercive citation requests—and examines the collateral damage to those caught in the crossfire of academic publishing. This issue threatens the integrity of peer review, a cornerstone of scientific validation, and could erode trust in published research. It affects researchers, editors, and publishers worldwide, potentially leading to increased retractions and a polluted scientific literature. Review mills involve coercive citation requests, where reviewers demand authors cite their own or affiliated work, often as a condition for acceptance. The article discusses the 'war' between these practices and efforts to combat them, leaving legitimate researchers caught in the middle.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 3, 00:00

**Background**: Peer review is a process where experts evaluate manuscripts before publication to ensure quality and validity. Review mills are a form of paper mill, which are unethical operations that produce fake or low-quality research or manipulate the review process. Coercive citation is a recognized form of citation manipulation, alongside excessive self-citation and citation cartels, and is addressed by publishers like Wiley.

## [Venus's Rift Valleys Hint at Ongoing Tectonic Activity](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02372-0) ⭐️ 5.0/10

A new study published in Nature suggests that Venus's steep rift valleys may be evidence of ongoing tectonic activity, challenging the long-held view that the planet is geologically dormant. The research, based on data from NASA's Magellan mission, identifies 'rift flanks'—ridges on the edges of rift valleys—that resemble features on Earth associated with active tectonics. This finding could reshape our understanding of Venus's geological evolution and its similarity to early Earth, providing a natural laboratory for studying tectonics without plate boundaries. It also informs upcoming missions like NASA's VERITAS, which will further investigate Venus's surface and interior. The rift valleys on Venus can span up to 10,000 kilometers (6,200 miles), comparable to Earth's East African Rift. The study focuses on 'rift flanks,' which are ridges formed on the edges of active or recently active rift valleys, similar to features like South Africa's Great Escarpment. The timing of these rifts' formation remains uncertain, but the presence of young rift flanks suggests recent activity.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 3, 00:00

**Background**: Venus has a thick atmosphere and a surface hidden by clouds, making direct observation difficult. Unlike Earth, Venus lacks plate tectonics, but it has extensive rift valley systems formed by crustal stretching. Previous studies have suggested possible tectonic activity, but this new evidence strengthens the case. NASA's Magellan mission, which mapped Venus in the early 1990s, provided the data used in this study.
