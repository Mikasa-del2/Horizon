A new study published in Nature on August 26, 2026, used single-cell RNA sequencing to show that cell-type-specific expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) account for a substantial portion of complex trait heritability. This highlights the critical role of cell-type-specific gene regulation in linking genetic variants to traits. This finding reshapes our understanding of the genetic architecture of complex traits, emphasizing that gene regulation must be studied at the cell-type level rather than in bulk tissues. It has major implications for identifying causal variants and mechanisms underlying diseases, potentially guiding future therapeutic targets and precision medicine approaches. The study leveraged single-cell RNA-sequencing to map eQTLs across diverse cell types, revealing that many eQTLs are cell-type-specific and that these variants contribute disproportionately to heritability. Single-cell RNA sequencing now allows researchers to profile gene expression in individual cells, enabling the identification of eQTLs that act only in specific cell types. Complex traits, such as height or disease susceptibility, are influenced by many genetic variants, and understanding how these variants affect gene regulation in relevant cell types is crucial for deciphering their biological mechanisms.

## [Single polyatomic ion absorption spectroscopy via photon recoil](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10915-8) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrated non-destructive single-molecule absorption spectroscopy of a polyatomic molecular ion by detecting photon recoil, achieving single-photon sensitivity. The work was published in Nature on August 26, 2026. This breakthrough enables absorption spectroscopy on individual polyatomic ions, which was previously challenging due to weak signals. It opens new avenues for studying molecular structure and dynamics at the single-molecule level, with implications for analytical chemistry and quantum science. The technique detects the photon recoil imparted on a co-trapped logic ion, amplifying the signal from a few absorbed photons to thousands of fluorescence photons. This method extends quantum logic spectroscopy to fast dipole-allowed transitions and enables single-photon-sensitive measurements of molecular vibrational transitions.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 26, 00:00

**Background**: Photon recoil spectroscopy is a technique that extracts signals from the recoil imparted by spectroscopy light on trapped ions. It exploits the efficient detection of phonons in ion crystals, making it suitable for precision spectroscopy. Single-molecule absorption spectroscopy has been a long-sought goal, as it allows direct probing of molecular structure without the need for fluorescence.

## [Quantum Oscillations Map g-Wave Altermagnetic Order in CrSb](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10902-z) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers used magnetic quantum oscillation measurements to map the three-dimensional bulk-resolved g-wave altermagnetic order parameter in CrSb, establishing it as a prototypical unconventional magnet with momentum-dependent spin splitting. The findings were published online in Nature on August 26, 2026. This work provides the first bulk-sensitive, three-dimensional mapping of an altermagnetic order parameter, offering a new experimental tool to characterize unconventional magnets. It deepens understanding of altermagnetism, a recently identified class of magnetic materials with potential applications in spintronics and fundamental physics. The quantum oscillation measurements resolve the momentum-dependent spin splitting in CrSb, revealing a g-wave symmetry of the order parameter. CrSb is a metallic altermagnet with a Néel temperature of 733 K, and previous studies have observed large band splitting and topological Berry phase effects.

rss · Nature - Latest Research · Aug 26, 00:00

**Background**: Altermagnetism is a recently proposed class of magnetic order that combines properties of ferromagnets and antiferromagnets, featuring spin-split electronic bands without net magnetization. Quantum oscillations, such as the de Haas-van Alphen effect, are sensitive to the Fermi surface geometry and can reveal the momentum-dependent spin splitting characteristic of altermagnets. CrSb has emerged as a model altermagnet due to its high Néel temperature and large spin splitting.
